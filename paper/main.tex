\begin{abstract}
Offline reinforcement learning usually treats logged rewards as fully observed or ignorably missing. We study finite-horizon Markov decision processes in which states, actions, and transitions are recorded, but reward observation may depend on the latent reward itself. Without additional restrictions, even the optimal action is not identified from infinite data. We introduce an odds-ratio sensitivity model indexed by $\Gamma\geq1$ and derive closed-form \emph{sharp} reward intervals for binary outcomes. Rectangular propagation gives sharp policy-value bounds, but comparing two separately robust values is unnecessarily conservative. We derive an exact baseline-relative bound whose gain is precisely the reward uncertainty weighted by candidate--baseline occupancy overlap, and optimize it by a linear program over occupancy measures. We also characterize the exact infinite-data ambiguity--regret tradeoff: when the sharp improvement interval crosses zero, every binary candidate-versus-baseline rule has positive worst-case regret, and a uniformly safe rule must essentially abstain. One-sided exact confidence limits yield a certificate valid simultaneously for all data-dependent policies, along with robust-regret and estimated-transition guarantees. Small tabular experiments validate the predicted sensitivity threshold, post-selection safety, and the necessity of correctly specifying $\Gamma$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Offline reinforcement learning (RL) seeks policy improvement using a fixed log rather than new interaction. This is attractive in health care, public policy, and recommendation, where exploration is costly. Yet outcomes in these applications are often selectively recorded: a conversion is known only after a user returns, a clinical outcome only after follow-up, and an adverse event only if it triggers reporting. If recording depends on the latent outcome, rewards are \emph{missing not at random} (MNAR), so complete-case averages can remain biased with unlimited data \citep{little2019statistical}.

Recent work obtains point identification for off-policy evaluation with MNAR rewards by treating future states as shadow variables and solving bridge equations \citep{wei2026mnar}. Such assumptions can be powerful, but their completeness and exclusion conditions are not empirically testable in general. We ask the complementary question:
\begin{quote}
\emph{What policy improvement is justified when rewards are not point identified, but the strength of selective recording is bounded?}
\end{quote}

We answer through partial identification and sensitivity analysis \citep{manski2003partial,rosenbaum2002observational}. A parameter $\Gamma$ bounds how much the reward-recording odds may differ between positive and negative outcomes. Rather than impute a single unverifiable reward model, we optimize against every model compatible with the observed law and the chosen $\Gamma$.

Our contributions are fourfold. First, we prove nonidentification without restrictions and derive sharp binary reward intervals, with a finite-support LP extension. Second, we characterize sharp fixed-policy values and sharp improvement over a baseline. Direct comparison cancels common uncertainty; the exact gain over subtracting separate value bounds is occupancy overlap times interval width. Third, we give an exact occupancy-measure LP and characterize the infinite-data ambiguity--regret tradeoff, establishing when abstention is information-theoretically unavoidable. Fourth, one-sided exact confidence limits produce a single reward set valid for every data-dependent policy, yielding post-selection-safe improvement, a finite-sample rate, and an estimated-transition extension. All validations use exact tabular planning and require no GPU.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Missing rewards and partial supervision.}
Offline RL with unlabeled transitions has been studied under reward-label scarcity \citep{yu2022unlabeled}, while MAHALO combines offline RL with observation-only data \citep{li2023mahalo}. Missing-outcome bandits study online learning when the probability of observing an outcome may depend on its value \citep{mahrooghi2025missing}; monitored MDPs make reward observability part of online control \citep{kazemipour2025monitored}. Partial identification has also been used in offline dynamic pricing under demand ambiguity \citep{bian2024pricing}. Most closely, \citet{wei2026mnar} point-identify MNAR policy values using future-state shadow variables. We instead permit nonidentification and optimize a policy directly over the sharp sensitivity set induced by a fixed offline log.

\paragraph{Sensitivity and robust policy learning.}
Sensitivity analysis and partial identification quantify conclusions under unverifiable selection assumptions \citep{rosenbaum2002observational,manski2003partial}. Robust policy learning under hidden action confounding has been studied in contextual and sequential settings \citep{kallus2018confounding,kallus2020confounding,jin2025policy,hess2026sharp}. Here actions and transitions are observed; the reward itself is selectively missing. This structure yields cellwise sharp sets and a baseline-relative occupancy program.

\paragraph{Safe and robust RL.}
High-confidence policy improvement and SPIBB protect a baseline against finite-sample or coverage error \citep{thomas2015high,laroche2019spibb}. Robust MDPs optimize over exogenously specified uncertainty sets \citep{iyengar2005robust,nilim2005robust,wiesemann2013robust}. Our ambiguity set is derived sharply from the MNAR observed law, and our direct objective exploits uncertainty shared by candidate and baseline rather than robustifying them separately.

\paragraph{Decision under partial identification.}
Minimax-regret treatment rules formalize decisions when observationally equivalent models recommend different actions, including the classical missing-outcome setting \citep{manski2007missing,manski2009diversified,stoye2012minimax}. Our ambiguity theorem specializes this logic to the sharp \emph{candidate--baseline improvement interval} generated by a sequential occupancy contrast. The new connection is operational: the same sharp interval determines an exact occupancy optimization problem, a safe-deployment rule, and the residual decision regret that remains even after infinite data.

\section{Setup and Sensitivity Model}
Consider an episodic MDP with horizon $H$, finite state space $\cS$, finite action space $\cA$, initial distribution $\mu$, transition kernels $P_h$, and binary rewards $R_h\in\{0,1\}$. For the base presentation, $(R_h,M_h)$ is conditionally independent of $S_{h+1}$ given $(S_h,A_h)$. If rewards depend on the realized transition, one may instead define every reward cell as $x=(h,s,a,s')$ and replace its occupancy by $d_h^\pi(s,a)P_h(s'\mid s,a)$; all bounds and the occupancy LP remain linear and unchanged in form. A Markov policy $\pi$ has occupancy
\[
 d_h^\pi(s,a)=\Prb_\pi(S_h=s,A_h=a)
\]
and value $V_r^\pi=\sum_{h,s,a}d_h^\pi(s,a)r_h(s,a)$, where $r_h(s,a)=\E[R_h\mid S_h=s,A_h=a]$.

Let $M_h\in\{0,1\}$ indicate reward observation. Each logged transition contains
\[
(S_h,A_h,S_{h+1},M_h,M_hR_h).
\]
Write $x=(h,s,a)$ and define the population observables on behavior-supported cells
\[
q_x=\Prb(M=1\mid x),\qquad
p_x=\Prb(R=1\mid M=1,x),
\]
and the unobserved missing-case success probability $\nu_x=\Prb(R=1\mid M=0,x)$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 r_x=q_xp_x+(1-q_x)\nu_x. \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
We assume $q_x>0$ whenever $p_x$ is invoked. A cell with zero behavior occupancy, or no reward observations in finite samples, receives the vacuous interval $[0,1]$. Thus ordinary offline-RL support limitations are represented explicitly rather than hidden inside extrapolation. Boundary odds are interpreted by continuity.

\begin{assumption}[Odds-ratio sensitivity]\label{ass:gamma}
For a user-specified $\Gamma\geq1$, every cell satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Gamma^{-1}\leq
\frac{\odds(M=1\mid R=1,x)}{\odds(M=1\mid R=0,x)}
\leq\Gamma, \label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
and the full reward ambiguity set is rectangular across cells.
\end{assumption}
At $\Gamma=1$, recording is conditionally independent of reward. Larger $\Gamma$ permits stronger selection. The parameter is a sensitivity input, not estimable from the MNAR observed law alone.

\section{Sharp Identification}
Without a restriction such as Assumption~\ref{ass:gamma}, two actions can have the same observed $(M,MR)$ distribution while their missing rewards reverse the optimal action.

\begin{proposition}[Nonidentification]\label{prop:nonid}
Policy values and the optimal policy are not identified without restrictions on reward-dependent missingness, even in a one-state, two-action, horizon-one problem with known dynamics and infinite data.
\end{proposition}

Define
\begin{equation}
L_\Gamma(p)=\frac{p}{p+\Gamma(1-p)},\qquad
U_\Gamma(p)=\frac{\Gamma p}{1-p+\Gamma p}. \label{eq:LU}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp cellwise bounds]\label{thm:cell}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:gamma},
\[
\nu_x\in[L_\Gamma(p_x),U_\Gamma(p_x)]
\]
and the sharp identified interval for the mean reward is
\begin{align}
\ell_x&=q_xp_x+(1-q_x)L_\Gamma(p_x),\label{eq:lower}\\
u_x&=q_xp_x+(1-q_x)U_\Gamma(p_x).\label{eq:upper}
\end{align}
Every point in $[\ell_x,u_x]$ is attained by a full-data distribution that reproduces the observed law and satisfies \eqref{eq:gamma}.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Bayes' rule gives
\[
\frac{\odds(M=1\mid R=1,x)}{\odds(M=1\mid R=0,x)}
=\frac{\odds(p_x)}{\odds(\nu_x)}.
\]
Inverting the odds bounds yields \eqref{eq:LU}; substituting into \eqref{eq:decomp} gives the endpoints. Any interior $\nu_x$ constructs a compatible four-cell table, proving sharpness. Full proofs are in the supplement.

The irreducible width is
\begin{equation}
w_x=(1-q_x)\{U_\Gamma(p_x)-L_\Gamma(p_x)\}. \label{eq:width}
\end{equation}
It grows with missingness and $\Gamma$ and persists at infinite sample size. At $q_x=1$ or $\Gamma=1$ it vanishes; at unobserved cells it becomes vacuous. Thus data quantity can shrink sampling uncertainty but cannot, by itself, shrink $w_x$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite reward alphabet]\label{prop:finite-support}
Let $R\in\{z_1,\ldots,z_K\}$, with observed-case probabilities $p_k$ and missing-case probabilities $v_k$. Pairwise recording-odds sensitivity is equivalent to the linear constraints
\[
p_kv_j\leq\Gamma p_jv_k,\qquad p_jv_k\leq\Gamma p_kv_j
\]
for all $j,k$, together with $v$ in the simplex. Minimizing and maximizing the full reward mean over this polytope gives sharp endpoints.
\end{proposition}
This extension supports ordinal or discretized outcomes without changing the downstream policy optimization.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fixed-policy values]\label{thm:value}
For a fixed policy, the sharp value interval is
\begin{equation}
\underline V^\pi=\sum_xd_x^\pi\ell_x,\qquad
\overline V^\pi=\sum_xd_x^\pi u_x. \label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
Consequently, absolute maximin planning is ordinary planning with reward $\ell$.
\end{theorem}
Absolute maximin planning protects absolute value against the reward set, but it need not improve a trusted baseline because it ignores where the baseline shares the same uncertainty.

\section{Sharp Safe Policy Improvement}
Let $\pi_b$ be a baseline and $c_x^\pi=d_x^\pi-d_x^{\pi_b}$. Write $z_+=\max\{z,0\}$ and $z_-=\min\{z,0\}$. Define the worst-case improvement
\[
J_\Gamma(\pi;\pi_b)=\inf_{r\in\cR_\Gamma}\{V_r^\pi-V_r^{\pi_b}\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Contrastive identification and cancellation]\label{thm:contrast}
For every policy, the sharp identified interval for improvement has endpoints
\begin{align}
\underline\Delta_\Gamma(\pi,\pi_b)
&=\sum_x\{\pos{c_x^\pi}\ell_x+\negp{c_x^\pi}u_x\},\label{eq:contrast}\\
\overline\Delta_\Gamma(\pi,\pi_b)
&=\sum_x\{\pos{c_x^\pi}u_x+\negp{c_x^\pi}\ell_x\}.\label{eq:contrast-upper}
\end{align}
Its width is the \emph{contrastive ambiguity}
\begin{equation}
\overline\Delta_\Gamma-\underline\Delta_\Gamma
=\sum_x|c_x^\pi|w_x. \label{eq:contrast-width}
\end{equation}
Writing $J_\Gamma=\underline\Delta_\Gamma$, we also have
\begin{equation}
J_\Gamma(\pi;\pi_b)=
\underline V^\pi-\overline V^{\pi_b}
+\sum_x\min\{d_x^\pi,d_x^{\pi_b}\}w_x. \label{eq:cancel}
\end{equation}
Thus direct comparison dominates separate robust values by exactly the uncertainty width on shared occupancy.
\end{theorem}

The interval reveals a three-way decision: $\underline\Delta_\Gamma>0$ certifies improvement, $\overline\Delta_\Gamma<0$ certifies inferiority, and an interval crossing zero is genuinely unresolved. In particular, two absolute policy values may be wide while their difference is point identified: Equation~\eqref{eq:contrast-width} vanishes whenever candidate and baseline have identical occupancy on every uncertain cell. Because both endpoints are attained, $J_\Gamma>0$ is \emph{necessary and sufficient} for strict improvement under every compatible reward model. If it is nonpositive, sharpness supplies a compatible model under which the candidate fails to improve.

\paragraph{Exact optimization.}
Let $\cD(P,\mu)$ be the finite-horizon occupancy polytope. Introduce $t_x$ and solve
\begin{align}
\max_{d\in\cD,t}\quad &\sum_xt_x \label{eq:lp}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
t_x&\leq(d_x-d_x^{\pi_b})\ell_x,\nonumber\\
t_x&\leq(d_x-d_x^{\pi_b})u_x.\nonumber
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Exact occupancy LP]\label{thm:lp}
The optimum of \eqref{eq:lp} equals $\max_\pi J_\Gamma(\pi;\pi_b)$. Normalizing a feasible occupancy within each state recovers a randomized Markov policy. Since $d^{\pi_b}$ is feasible, the optimum is nonnegative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\columnwidth}
\textbf{Sharp MNAR Safe Improvement.} Given $P$, baseline $\pi_b$, $\Gamma$, and confidence level $1-\delta$: (1) estimate $q_x,p_x$ and construct simultaneous outer reward intervals; (2) compute $d^{\pi_b}$; (3) solve \eqref{eq:lp}; (4) subtract any transition-estimation penalty; (5) deploy the recovered policy only if the remaining certificate is strictly positive, otherwise return $\pi_b$.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}
The population version uses the sharp intervals directly. For a fixed candidate, $J_\Gamma$ is nonincreasing in $\Gamma$, so the critical value $\Gamma^\star=\sup\{\Gamma:J_\Gamma>0\}$ summarizes robustness and can be found by bisection. Unlike a hyperparameter selected for return, $\Gamma$ indexes a family of scientific assumptions and should be reported as a curve.

\paragraph{Why the LP and cancellation are exact.}
For a fixed occupancy contrast, rectangularity separates the adversary's problem by cell. A positive coefficient is minimized at $\ell_x$ and a negative coefficient at $u_x$, which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:contrast}; reversing the endpoints gives Equation~\eqref{eq:contrast-upper}. Subtracting the separate-value lower bound cell by cell leaves $d_x^{\pi_b}w_x$ when the candidate visits the cell more often and $d_x^\pi w_x$ otherwise, yielding Equation~\eqref{eq:cancel}. Finally, the scalar term in Equation~\eqref{eq:contrast} equals the minimum of two affine functions of $d_x$. Its hypograph is exactly the pair of inequalities in \eqref{eq:lp}. Standard occupancy-flow constraints characterize all randomized Markov policies, so no relaxation gap is introduced. This argument also explains why randomization can be useful: the robust objective is concave and piecewise linear in occupancy, and an interior occupancy can balance adverse reward assignments that would punish either deterministic policy.

\subsection{Why abstention is unavoidable}
The following result separates irreducible ambiguity from finite-sample conservatism.

\begin{theorem}[Exact ambiguity--regret tradeoff]\label{thm:minimax}
Suppose the sharp identified interval for candidate-minus-baseline value is $[L,U]$ with $L<0<U$. Any possibly randomized rule choosing between these two policies deploys the candidate with probability $\alpha$, common to every compatible model, and has worst-case simple regret
\begin{equation}
\max\{\alpha(-L),(1-\alpha)U\}. \label{eq:minimax}
\end{equation}
The minimax choice is $\alpha^\star=U/(U-L)$, with regret
\[
\frac{(-L)U}{U-L}.
\]
A deterministic selector has minimax regret $\min\{-L,U\}$. Furthermore, any selector that is no worse than the baseline with probability at least $1-\delta$ in every compatible model must satisfy $\alpha\leq\delta$.
\end{theorem}

This is the contrastive sequential specialization of classical minimax-regret logic for missing outcomes \citep{manski2007missing}. For a symmetric interval of width $W$, randomized and deterministic minimax regret are $W/4$ and $W/2$. In the symmetric binary cell $p=1/2$ with observation rate $q$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cell} gives
$W=(1-q)(\Gamma-1)/(\Gamma+1)$, making the irreducible price of selective recording explicit.

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Sharpness supplies two full-data models with the same observed law and improvement $L$ and $U$. Any binary deployment rule therefore uses the same candidate probability $\alpha$ in both. Its regrets are $\alpha(-L)$ and $(1-\alpha)U$; equalizing them gives the minimax expression. Under the lower-endpoint model, selecting the candidate is the unsafe event, so a $(1-\delta)$ uniform safety guarantee forces $\alpha\leq\delta$. The result applies to arbitrary horizons by embedding the ambiguous choice at the first step and making later rewards action independent. Thus robust abstention can be information-theoretically necessary rather than an artifact of our LP.

\section{Finite-Sample Safe Policy Selection}
From $n$ independent trajectories, let $N_x$ be visits to cell $x$, $O_x$ observed rewards, and $Y_x$ observed positive rewards. Conditional on visits,
$O_x\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N_x,q_x)$ and
$Y_x\mid O_x\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(O_x,p_x)$.

For $D=H|\cS||\cA|$, allocate $\delta/(3D)$ to three one-sided exact Clopper--Pearson statements per cell \citep{clopper1934fiducial}: $q_x\geq q_x^L$, $p_x\geq p_x^L$, and $p_x\leq p_x^U$. Only these three tails are needed. Define
\begin{align}
\widehat\ell_x&=q_x^Lp_x^L+(1-q_x^L)L_\Gamma(p_x^L),\label{eq:hatl}\\
\widehat u_x&=q_x^Lp_x^U+(1-q_x^L)U_\Gamma(p_x^U).\label{eq:hatu}
\end{align}
The same lower limit $q^L$ appears twice because the lower reward endpoint increases with $q$, whereas the upper endpoint decreases with $q$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform outer identification]\label{thm:finite}
With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every cell,
$[\ell_x,u_x]\subseteq[\widehat\ell_x,\widehat u_x]$.
On this event, the contrastive objective over the empirical outer set is a valid lower bound for every policy simultaneously. Consequently, optimizing it on the same data and returning the baseline unless the optimum is positive yields
\[
\Prb\{V^{\widehat\pi}\geq V^{\pi_b}\}\geq1-\delta.
\]
\end{theorem}

The simultaneous statement is essential: it remains valid after searching over arbitrarily many policies representable by the occupancy polytope. No minimum behavior coverage is required for validity. If a cell has no usable observations, its reward interval is simply $[0,1]$, so the method becomes conservative rather than unsound. Coverage enters the rate and deployment power, not the logical guarantee.

\paragraph{Proof idea.}
Conditional exact coverage and a union bound establish the three nuisance inequalities in every cell. The lower reward endpoint is increasing in $q$ and $p$, while the upper endpoint is decreasing in $q$ and increasing in $p$; hence Equations~\eqref{eq:hatl}--\eqref{eq:hatu} contain the entire population rectangle. Taking an infimum over a larger set can only decrease a policy's certificate. Crucially, this containment is simultaneous before optimization, so it applies to the policy returned by the same data-dependent LP without a second holdout set. The baseline occupancy is feasible with objective zero; positive empirical optimum therefore implies positive true improvement on the coverage event, while a nonpositive optimum triggers exact fallback.

Let $\rho=\max_x\max\{\ell_x-\widehat\ell_x,\widehat u_x-u_x\}$. If $\pi_\Gamma^\star$ maximizes the population objective and $\widehat\pi$ maximizes the empirical one, then on the coverage event
\begin{equation}
J_\Gamma(\pi_\Gamma^\star)-J_\Gamma(\widehat\pi)\leq2H\rho. \label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
For clipped Hoeffding analogues with minimum visit and observed-reward counts $N_{\min}$ and $O_{\min}$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
J_\Gamma(\pi_\Gamma^\star)-J_\Gamma(\widehat\pi)
\leq4H\!\left[
\sqrt{\frac{\log(4D/\delta)}{2N_{\min}}}
+\Gamma\sqrt{\frac{\log(4D/\delta)}{2O_{\min}}}
\right]. \label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
Thus coverage controls power rather than validity: unvisited cells simply remain vacuous.

\begin{proposition}[Estimated transitions]\label{prop:transition}
Suppose $\widehat P$ obeys
$\max_{s,a}\|P_h(\cdot\mid s,a)-\widehat P_h(\cdot\mid s,a)\|_1\leq\eta_h$.
For rewards in $[0,1]$, a contrastive certificate computed under $\widehat P$ remains valid under $P$ after subtracting
\begin{equation}
B_{\rm imp}=\sum_{h=1}^{H-1}(H-h)\eta_h. \label{eq:transition}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
The continuation-value span after time $h$ is at most $H-h$; total variation changes its expectation by at most $\eta_h(H-h)/2$. Summing by backward induction bounds each policy value, and applying the result to candidate and baseline yields \eqref{eq:transition}. Multinomial $\ell_1$ concentration supplies simultaneous data-dependent $\eta_h$ \citep{weissman2003inequalities}. As with reward coverage, an unseen transition row can be assigned the vacuous maximum distance two: safety survives, while power disappears.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
Our sharpness results use rectangular reward ambiguity and tabular contexts. Rectangularity is deliberately conservative: it permits each cell's missingness mechanism to vary independently. Scientific restrictions tying mechanisms across time or state can tighten identification, but may produce nonrectangular dynamic ambiguity and a harder optimization problem. Function approximation introduces extrapolation error distinct from MNAR ambiguity; a practical large-scale method should preserve this separation rather than hide both inside one uncertainty bonus.

Transition-conditioned rewards can be handled by enlarging the cell to $(h,s,a,s')$, but this increases the number of conditionals that need reward observations. Future-state shadow variables can instead couple cells and tighten the set. One promising hybrid is to intersect approximate bridge restrictions with the $\Gamma$ set, recovering point identification when the bridge assumptions are exact and degrading continuously when they are not. The present method also assumes that the reward-observation indicator is correctly logged; misrecorded missingness would require an additional error model. Finally, $\Gamma$ must be calibrated from domain knowledge, targeted audits, or external data. Our misspecification experiment shows why optimizing it by the same observed likelihood would invalidate the intended guarantee.

When rewards are MNAR, a unique optimal policy may not be supported by the log even with infinite data. Sharp partial identification exposes this ambiguity, direct baseline comparison removes only the uncertainty that truly matters, and simultaneous confidence sets preserve safety after policy search. The resulting method is computationally small but decision-theoretically exact: it improves when improvement is justified and abstains when no uniformly safe conclusion exists.
